How it works

Monthly payment = amount × r ÷ (1 − (1+r)−n), where r is APR ÷ 12 and n is months. Extra payments go straight to principal after that month's interest, which is why they cut total interest and shorten the loan.

Worked example (defaults): $25,000 at 8.5% for 60 months = $513 per month, $5,775 interest, $30,775 total.

Frequently asked questions

What does the extra payment do?

Each extra dollar goes to principal after that month's interest, so total interest falls and the loan ends earlier.

Is this for any loan type?

Yes, any fixed-rate amortising loan: auto, personal or student, as long as the rate and term are fixed.
