How Much to Tip in the US? A Simple 2026 Tipping Guide

Tipping in the United States can feel confusing, especially for visitors. There is no single federal law that sets a tip amount for customers. Instead, tipping follows common custom, and the amount usually depends on the type of service and how good it was.

This guide explains the typical ranges people use, so you can decide quickly and fairly. It is general information only, not a rule you must follow.

1. Restaurants: the most common case

For sit-down restaurants in the US, a typical tip is 15% to 20% of the pre-tax bill for standard to good service. Many people use 18% as a simple middle point, 15% when service was basic, and 20% or more when service was excellent.

Example: on a $50 pre-tax bill, 15% is $7.50, 18% is $9.00, and 20% is $10.00.

Check the bill first: some restaurants, especially for large groups, already add a service charge or gratuity. If it is already included, you do not need to tip twice unless you want to add extra.

2. Food delivery

For restaurant delivery, a common range is 15% to 20%, or a few dollars more for a small order, bad weather, or a long distance. Delivery fees paid to the app are not the same as a tip for the driver, so check whether a tip was added.

3. Bars and coffee shops

At a bar, $1 to $2 per drink is a common custom, or 15% to 20% of the tab. At a coffee shop, tipping is optional and often smaller, for example rounding up or leaving $1. Tip jars and card prompts do not mean a large tip is required.

4. Taxis and rideshares

For taxis and rideshare trips, 10% to 20% is typical, with more for help with heavy bags or a difficult trip. Most rideshare apps let you add a tip after the ride.

5. How to split a bill fairly

When splitting a bill, first split the food total, then add tax and tip on each person's share. The simplest fair method is: each person pays for what they ordered, plus their share of tax and the agreed tip percentage. Our free tip calculator does this math in seconds.

Quick tipping table

  • Restaurants (sit-down): 15%–20%
  • Food delivery: 15%–20%
  • Bars: $1–$2 per drink, or 15%–20%
  • Coffee shops: optional, often $1 or rounding up
  • Taxis and rideshares: 10%–20%

Frequently asked questions

Should I tip on the pre-tax or post-tax total?

Custom in the US is usually to tip on the pre-tax amount, but tipping on the total is also common and is not wrong. Pick one method and be consistent.

Is tipping required by law?

No. Tipping is a social custom in the US, not a legal requirement for customers, except when a restaurant clearly states a service charge on the bill. That charge is different from a voluntary tip.

What if service was poor?

You can tip less, and you can politely tell the manager what went wrong. A very low tip alone does not explain the problem, so a short, polite comment helps more.
