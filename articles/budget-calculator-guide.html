Budget Calculator Guide: The 50/30/20 Rule With a Simple Example

A budget is simply a plan for money you already have. The 50/30/20 rule is a popular starting point: split your take-home pay into 50% for needs, 30% for wants, and 20% for savings. It is a starting point, not a rule you must follow exactly.

This guide is general information only, not personal financial advice, with a worked example you can check by hand.

1. Worked example: $4,000 take-home pay

  • Needs, 50%: 50% of $4,000 = $2,000 — rent, groceries, utilities, transport, and minimum payments.
  • Wants, 30%: 30% of $4,000 = $1,200 — eating out, streaming, hobbies, and non-essential shopping.
  • Savings, 20%: 20% of $4,000 = $800 — an emergency fund, debt above the minimum, and long-term saving.

Check: $2,000 + $1,200 + $800 = $4,000, so the three parts add up to the full take-home pay.

Quick check: 10% of $4,000 is $400. Multiply by 5 for 50% ($2,000), by 3 for 30% ($1,200), and by 2 for 20% ($800).

2. Start with one honest month

Before changing anything, write down one real month of spending. Put each item into needs, wants, or savings. Most people find one or two surprises — often a subscription, delivery fees, or small daily purchases — and fixing one of those is easier than rebuilding the whole budget at once.

3. Pay yourself first, then limit wants

Move the savings part on payday, before the rest is spent. For wants, set a limit instead of a ban: a limit you can keep is better than a strict rule you abandon after a week. Check once a week for 10 minutes, and fix one mistake in the next month rather than trying to fix everything at once.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as a need and what counts as a want?

Needs are costs you must pay to live and work: housing, basic groceries, utilities, transport, and minimum debt payments. Wants are choices that make life nicer but are not required: eating out, extra streaming services, and upgraded phones or clothes.

What if my needs are already more than 50%?

That is common in high-rent cities. Treat 50/30/20 as a target to move toward, not a pass-or-fail test. Start by moving a small fixed amount to savings on payday, then adjust one wants category at a time.

Is this personal financial advice?

No. This is general information and a worked example only. Your rent, debts, taxes, and family situation change the right split for you.
