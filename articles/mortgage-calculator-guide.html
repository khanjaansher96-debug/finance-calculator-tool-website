Mortgage Guide: Your Real Monthly Payment, Not Just Principal and Interest

Most first-time buyers make the same mistake: they shop for a house by the principal-and-interest payment, then get surprised by the real monthly bill. Property tax, home insurance, HOA dues, and sometimes PMI are added on top, and together they can add hundreds of dollars a month. This guide works through one complete example so you can see the full picture before you fall in love with a house.

This guide is general information only, not mortgage or financial advice. Rates, taxes, and insurance vary by location and lender.

1. Worked example: $425,000 home, 20% down

  • Home price: $425,000
  • Down payment (20%): $85,000
  • Loan amount: $425,000 − $85,000 = $340,000
  • Rate: 6.75% fixed, 30 years (360 payments), monthly rate 0.5625%
  • Principal and interest: $2,205.23 per month
  • Total principal and interest over 30 years: $793,884.07
  • Total interest alone: $453,884.07
Read that last line twice: On a $340,000 loan at 6.75% for 30 years, interest costs more than the loan itself. That is normal for a long mortgage — and it is why a bigger down payment or a shorter term saves so much.

2. What gets added on top

Your lender usually collects these monthly into escrow, on top of principal and interest:

  • Property tax — set by your county, often 0.5%–2% of the home's value per year.
  • Home insurance — required by the lender, priced by location and coverage.
  • HOA dues — only if the home is in an association, and they can rise over time.
  • PMI — private mortgage insurance, usually required if your down payment is under 20%. With 20% down in this example, PMI is avoided.

On our own calculator, this same $425,000 home example works out at about $2,745 per month once typical tax, insurance, HOA, and PMI-style extras are included — about $540 a month above principal and interest alone. Your county's tax rate is the biggest variable, so check it before you budget.

3. Three ways to lower the payment honestly

Bigger down payment. Every extra $10,000 down cuts the loan — and the interest — directly. It also helps you avoid PMI.

Shorter term. A 15-year loan has a higher monthly payment but dramatically less total interest. Run both terms and compare total interest, not just the payment.

Buy slightly less house. A $25,000 lower price, at the same rate and term, cuts principal and interest by roughly $162 a month and tens of thousands in interest over 30 years.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my payment higher than principal and interest?

Because of escrow: tax, insurance, and any HOA or PMI are collected monthly by your lender. Ask for an itemised estimate, not just the loan payment.

Does a lower rate always beat a lower price?

Not always. A lower price cuts the loan, the tax base, and the insurance value at once. Compare the full monthly payment and total interest for each option.

Is this mortgage advice?

No. This is general information and a worked example only. A lender or mortgage adviser can quote your actual rate and costs.
