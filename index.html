How to use this mortgage payment calculator

Enter the home price, your down payment, interest rate and loan term. Add yearly property tax and home insurance, plus HOA dues if any, and the total monthly payment updates instantly. This is a mortgage payment calculator with taxes and insurance — not just principal & interest — so the result is closer to what you will actually pay each month.

Formula & worked example

Monthly principal & interest = P × r(1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where P = loan amount, r = monthly rate, n = months

Example (the defaults above): $425,000 home, 20% down ($85,000), loan $340,000 at 6.75% for 30 years → principal & interest $2,205/mo, plus $390 tax + $150 insurance = $2,745/mo total, with $453,884 total interest. Change any number and the result updates instantly using the same formula.

Understanding your results

The donut chart splits your payment into principal & interest, tax, insurance and HOA + PMI. The year-by-year schedule shows how much principal you pay off each year — early payments are mostly interest, later payments are mostly principal. If you are asking “how much house can I afford?”, a common guide is to keep the total housing payment near or below 28% of your gross monthly income, then confirm it fits your budget.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my payment higher than principal & interest? Most US mortgage payments also include property tax and insurance held in escrow, plus HOA and PMI when they apply.

When does PMI stop? Typically when your loan balance reaches 80% of the home's value (20% equity).

15-year vs 30-year mortgage — which is cheaper? A 15-year term has a higher monthly payment but far less total interest. Try both terms above and compare “Total interest over the loan”.

Does this include closing costs? No. Closing costs are one-time fees paid at purchase and are not part of the monthly payment.

Payment = P × r(1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1) — the standard amortization formula

Does an extra payment help? Yes — extra money goes to principal, so less interest accrues and the loan ends sooner. The result panel shows exactly how much you save.

Why does a longer term cost more? A longer term lowers the monthly payment but you pay interest for more months, so total interest rises.

How to use this compound interest calculator

Enter a starting amount, a monthly contribution, the years invested and an average annual return. The chart shows the balance by year, and the results split the final value into money you put in versus growth earned — the clearest way to see compound interest working over time, for example how much $10,000 plus $500 a month can grow in 10, 20 or 30 years.

Formula & worked example

Each month: balance = balance × (1 + annual rate ÷ 12) + monthly contribution

Example (defaults): $10,000 start + $500/mo at 8% for 20 years → you put in $130,000 and end with about $343,778 — $213,778 of growth.

Frequently asked questions

Is 8% realistic? It is a common long-term stock-market assumption, but real returns vary year to year and are never guaranteed.

Why does starting early matter so much? Growth itself earns growth. The last years of a long timeline usually add more than the first years, even with the same monthly contribution.

Does this include taxes or fees? No — this is growth before taxes and fees, so a real account may end lower.

How to use this income tax calculator 2025

Enter your gross annual income, filing status and any pre-tax deductions such as 401(k) or HSA contributions. You get federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, total tax, effective and marginal rates, and estimated take-home pay per month and per paycheck — a quick take-home pay calculator using 2025 tax brackets.

How this estimate works

Taxable income = gross income − pre-tax deductions − the 2025 standard deduction for your filing status. Federal tax is then applied progressively through the 2025 brackets (10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, 37%). Social Security is 6.2% up to the 2025 wage base of $176,100; Medicare is 1.45%.

Example (defaults): $85,000 single → federal tax $10,149, total tax $16,651, take-home $68,349, effective rate 19.6%, marginal bracket 22%.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between effective and marginal rate? Your marginal rate is the rate on your highest dollar of income. Your effective rate — total tax divided by total income — is almost always lower.

Does this include state tax? No. State tax is not included and varies by state. Credits are also not included.

This is an estimate only — credits, deductions and state tax can change your real result. Verify figures at IRS.gov.

How to use this 50/30/20 budget calculator

Enter your monthly take-home pay, then what you actually spend on needs (rent, bills, groceries, transport), wants (eating out, entertainment, travel) and savings. The calculator compares each category with its target and tells you which one is over or under — a simple monthly budget planner based on the 50/30/20 rule popularised for paycheck budgeting.

How the rule works

Targets are simple: needs target = 50% of take-home pay, wants = 30%, savings = 20%. Example (defaults): $5,200 take-home → targets $2,600 / $1,560 / $1,040. With $900 saved, your savings rate is 17.3%.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as a need vs a want? Needs are bills you must pay to live and work. Anything you could cut without serious hardship is a want, even if you buy it every month.

What if my rent alone is over 50%? That is common in high-cost cities. Use the rule as a direction, not a pass/fail test — lower wants first, and aim to raise the savings rate gradually.

How to use this tip calculator & bill split calculator

Enter the bill, tap a tip percentage (15%, 18%, 20% or 25%) or use the slider for a custom tip, then set how many people are splitting. You get the tip amount, the bill + tip total, and exactly what each person pays — the fastest way to split a restaurant bill fairly.

Formula & example

Tip = bill × tip %  ·  Per person = (bill + tip) ÷ people

Example (defaults): $86.40 bill, 20% tip, split 2 ways → tip $17.28, total $103.68, $51.84 per person.

How much should you tip in the USA?

15–20% is standard for table service, 20% or more for great service, around 10% for takeout with service, and $1–$2 per drink at a bar. Tipping is usually calculated on the pre-tax bill total.

How to use this percentage calculator

Three tools in one: (1) what is X% of Y — for discounts, tips and tax, (2) what percentage one number is of another, and (3) percentage increase or decrease from an old value to a new one. Type any numbers and all three results update instantly.

Formulas

X% of Y = Y × X ÷ 100  ·  Part as % = part ÷ whole × 100  ·  % change = (new − old) ÷ |old| × 100

Defaults check: 20% of 250 = $50 · 45 of 180 = 25% · 80 → 100 = +25% change.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a discount? Find X% of the price, then subtract it. Example: 20% of $250 is $50 off, so you pay $200 — shown automatically in tool 1.

Percentage points vs percent — what is the difference? A rate moving from 5% to 6% is +1 percentage point, but a 20% increase in the rate itself.

How to use this BMI calculator for adults

Enter your height in feet and inches and your weight in pounds. You get your BMI, its CDC category for adults, and the healthy weight range for your height — useful as a quick screening check before a fuller health conversation.

Formula & example

BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)²

Example (defaults): 5′9″ (69 in), 180 lb → BMI = 703 × 180 ÷ 69² = 26.6 — Overweight range. Healthy range for this height is 125–169 lb. BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnosis — talk to a clinician about your individual health.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI? For adults, the CDC categories are: under 18.5 underweight, 18.5–24.9 healthy, 25–29.9 overweight, and 30 or above obese.

Is BMI accurate for athletes? BMI does not separate muscle from fat, so very muscular people can score high without excess body fat. It is a screening measure, not a diagnosis.
